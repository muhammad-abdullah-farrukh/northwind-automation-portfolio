# Qwen3.5-4B and two rivals win 6 GB

**Three models fit your GTX 1660 SUPER and fit the job: Qwen3.5-4B (Q4_K_M, 3.4 GB), Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 (Q4_K_M, 2.5 GB) and Granite 4.1 3B (about 2 GB).** All three are Apache 2.0, run fully in VRAM with room for a long context, and are expected to generate roughly 25-35 t/s on this card (an estimate, since none was measured on a 1660). Qwen3.5-4B has the strongest vendor-reported instruction following (IFEval 89.8) and an independent extraction result of 96.60 F1 non-thinking on a noisy-string task. Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 is the safest runner-up: it has the only independent structured-output data (100% native schema validity and accuracy in a small 14-task study; the checkpoint tested is not stated in my notes), no thinking mode to manage, and the smallest footprint. Granite 4.1 3B is the lowest-risk tool-calling option on paper (BFCL v3 60.8) but trails on IFEval. Larger 7-9B models (Qwen3.5-9B, Granite 4.1 8B, Qwen3-8B) do not fit at Q4_K_M in about 5 GB. They need a 3-bit quant, whose tail errors threaten strict JSON, or CPU spill that cuts speed to roughly 5-12 t/s (estimate). **Almost every quality number below is vendor-reported, none comes from this hardware, and no public benchmark covers invoices, so the ranking is a shortlist to be settled by your own benchmark script, not a verdict.**

## What actually fits in about 5 GB

The budget is weights plus KV cache plus roughly 0.3-0.5 GB of CUDA compute buffer plus headroom for Windows, so weights should stay under about 4.2-4.5 GB (an estimate). Sizes below come from Ollama and Hugging Face file listings. Qwen3.5-4B is **3.4 GB at Q4_K_M and 5.3 GB at Q8**, so Q8 is out ([Ollama tags](https://ollama.com/library/qwen3.5/tags)). Qwen3-4B-2507 is **2.5 GB at Q4_K_M and 4.3 GB at Q8** ([Ollama tags](https://ollama.com/library/qwen3/tags), via search snippet). Granite 4 3B is **2.1 GB** on Ollama and the 3b-h hybrid is 1.9 GB ([Ollama tags](https://ollama.com/library/granite4/tags)). Phi-4-mini is 2.5 GB, Ministral 3 3B is 3.0 GB, and Gemma 3 4B is 3.3 GB at Q4_K_M ([Phi-4-mini](https://ollama.com/library/phi4-mini/tags), [Ministral 3](https://ollama.com/library/ministral-3/tags), [Gemma 3](https://ollama.com/library/gemma3/tags)).

Several newer models are excluded by size. Gemma 4 E4B is **6.6 GB at Q4_K_M** and E2B is 4.6 GB, which barely fits with a tiny context ([Ollama](https://ollama.com/library/gemma4/tags)). The 8-9B dense models are borderline: Qwen3-8B Q4_K_M is 5.03 GB and Qwen3.5-9B Q4_K_M is 5.68 GB, so only Q3 variants (Qwen3-8B Q3_K_M 4.12 GB, Qwen3.5-9B Q3_K_M 4.67 GB, which is tight) are plausible ([bartowski](https://huggingface.co/bartowski/Qwen_Qwen3-8B-GGUF), [Unsloth](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.5-9B-GGUF)). The 30-35B-A3B mixtures need 16-19 GB or more and are impractical with 16 GB of RAM ([Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen3.5/gguf-benchmarks)).

Q3 is a real cost for structured output. On a Qwen3.5 quant benchmark, mean perplexity moved only about 2% from Q4_K_M to Q3_K_M, but the 99.9th-percentile KL divergence roughly doubled (0.548 to 0.973); the model size in that extract is unclear ([Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen3.5/gguf-benchmarks)). Strict JSON is a tail-error problem, so a 4B at Q5/Q6 is a better use of 5 GB than an 8B at Q3 unless your test shows otherwise (inference). Quant behavior is also model-specific: Gemma 4 E4B had perplexity of 23.06 at Q4_K_M against 7.11 at BF16, and 919 at Q3_K_M ([llama.cpp #22407](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/22407)).

## Speed: nothing was measured on your card for the shortlist

No measured Ollama throughput exists in the notes for Qwen3.5, Qwen3-2507, Granite 4.x, Gemma or Phi-4-mini on a 1660-class GPU. The closest measured data, from a GPU-hosting vendor on a plain GTX 1660 with Ollama 0.5.11 in early 2025, shows **Llama 3.2 3B at 38.4 t/s, Qwen2.5 3B at 26.3 t/s, Qwen2.5 7B at 18.2 t/s and Llama 3.1 8B at 16.7 t/s** ([DatabaseMart](https://www.databasemart.com/blog/ollama-gpu-benchmark-gtx1660)). A conflicting source, LocalScore, reports Llama 3.1 8B Q4_K_M on the 1660 SUPER at only **9.6 t/s generation, 129 t/s prompt processing and 10.9 s time to first token** ([LocalScore](https://www.localscore.ai/model/1)). The notes suspect context length or layer spill explains the gap, but that is not confirmed. Decode is memory-bandwidth bound at 336 GB/s, so 3-4B Q4 models should land near the 3B figures above, about 25-35 t/s (inference, not measurement). Prompt processing is the quieter risk for extraction: llama.cpp measured about 150 t/s on a 7B Q4_0 on a 1660 ([llama.cpp #15013](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/15013)), so a long invoice prompt costs seconds before the first token.

On configuration, KV-cache quantization requires flash attention (`OLLAMA_FLASH_ATTENTION=1`, `OLLAMA_KV_CACHE_TYPE=q8_0`), roughly halves KV memory, and carries a reported perplexity cost of 0.002-0.05 ([ModelPiper](https://modelpiper.com/blog/ollama-kv-cache-quantization)). Flash attention gains about 4% on prompt processing on a 1660 and nothing on generation ([llama.cpp #15013](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/15013)). I found no confirmed 1660-specific Ollama bug. Spilling layers to the Ryzen 5 3600 is a cliff: roughly 5 t/s for 8B Q4 models on dual-channel DDR4 by one unfetched source ([promptquorum](https://www.promptquorum.com/local-llms/fastest-local-llms-low-end-pcs)).

## Where each of the top three stands

**Qwen3.5-4B** leads the 3-4B class on every vendor number retrieved: IFEval 89.8 against 83.4 and 82.30, and MMLU-Pro 79.1 against 69.6 ([card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B)). Its tool-calling figure is weaker on paper: BFCL-V4 50.3 is not comparable to its rivals' v3 scores, but the same card family reports 66.1 for the 9B, so the 4B sits well below its sibling. Three cautions apply. Its headline scores are thinking-mode figures and thinking is on by default, so a non-thinking run will score lower on knowledge benchmarks. A controlled study found no extraction difference between think and no-think templates (under 0.004 F1) ([arXiv 2606.08051](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08051v1)), so non-thinking is the right mode for latency and parser safety. The hybrid Gated DeltaNet architecture should keep KV small at long context, but that is an architectural inference, not a measurement. And Ollama had a bug where `think=false` combined with a `format` schema silently disabled the JSON constraint on Gemma 4, in the same class as an earlier Qwen3.5 issue ([ollama#15260](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/15260)). Test the exact flag combination on your Ollama version.

**Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507** is the evidence-heavy choice. It scores BFCL v3 61.9 and IFEval 83.4 from the vendor ([card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507)) and hit **100% native schema validity and 1.000 content accuracy** in the independent study, versus 78.6% validity for both Llama 3.2 sizes and 92.9% for Phi-4-mini ([arXiv 2609.23742](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23742v1)). The study's 14-task suite is small and the result looks like a ceiling effect, so treat it as supportive, not decisive. A leaderboard snippet gives BFCL overall 62.04% for Qwen3-4B (Prompt) but only 35.25% on multi-turn, consistent with small models being weak at multi-turn tool use ([BFCL changelog](https://github.com/ShishirPatil/gorilla/blob/main/berkeley-function-call-leaderboard/CHANGELOG.md), via search result, not verified on the official site). At 2.5 GB it leaves the most room for context on your card, and its vendor ceilings are lower than Qwen3.5-4B's.

**Granite 4.1 3B** is the smallest and the lowest-risk for tool calling, with BFCL v3 60.80 within about one point of Qwen3-4B-2507 and IFEval 82.30 ([card](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-4.1-3b)). It has no independent structured-output or extraction numbers. IBM's table shows the 8B reaching BFCL v3 68.27 and IFEval 87.06 ([Granite 4.1 blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/granite-4-1)), making Granite 4.1 8B the best-documented 8B for your use if you accept a 3-bit quant or partial offload. Your repo's earlier benchmark runs already used granite4.2:3b, so comparison against your own baseline is free. Aggregator BFCL-V4 listings put Granite 4.2 3B at 0.524 and LFM2.5-2.6B at 0.569, all self-reported and none verified ([llm-stats](https://llm-stats.com/benchmarks/bfcl-v4)).

**Why not the others.** Phi-4-mini trails on IFEval (70.1) and on independent native JSON validity (92.9%), though it reached 1.000 accuracy under constrained decoding ([Phi-4-mini report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.01743), [arXiv 2609.23742](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23742v1)). Ministral 3 3B claims native function calling and JSON output but publishes no IFEval or BFCL score on its card. Gemma 4 E4B does not fit. LFM2.5-1.2B has IFEval 86.23 at 1.17B parameters but BFCL v3 of only 49.12, a custom license (commercial terms unverified) and Pythonic tool-call output that may need parser support ([card](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct)). SmolLM3's BFCL figure of 92.3 comes from a vendor harness and is not comparable ([card](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM3-3B)). One blog claims BFCL v4 scores in the 80s for Qwen3-4B, Gemma 4 E4B and Phi-4-mini; it contradicts the model cards and is excluded ([Ertas](https://www.ertas.ai/blog/on-device-tool-calling-2026-qwen3-gemma4-phi4)).

## Gaps, unverified claims, and a local test plan

The evidence has large holes. No independent BFCL v4 numbers exist in my notes for the Qwen3.5 small models, and the official leaderboard CSV (`data_overall.csv` on gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu) was never read directly. No public benchmark compares the shortlist on invoices; an invoice-extraction repo exists whose leaderboard was not retrieved ([slm-doc-extract-bench](https://github.com/carlacasaroti/slm-doc-extract-bench)). No hallucination or Artificial Analysis data was gathered for sub-9B models. The Qwen3.5 release date (about February 2026) rests on weak metadata. Granite 4.1's Ollama tag, tool-support badges for Ministral, LFM and SmolLM3, and Llama 3.x and Ministral 8B licenses were not verified. No speed was measured for CPU-spilled models on a Ryzen 5 3600.

Constrained decoding is the strongest lever available. In the independent study it lifted every tested model to 100% schema validity and raised Llama-3B content accuracy from 0.777 to 0.991 ([arXiv 2609.23742](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23742v1)). Ollama applies a JSON schema passed in `format` to any model ([Ollama docs](https://docs.ollama.com/capabilities/structured-outputs)), so validity should not separate candidates; field-value accuracy will. Another independent benchmark reports that JSON validity often exceeds value accuracy ([SOB, arXiv 2604.25359](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25359)).

Your existing script, `evals/bench_llm.py`, already supports the plan. It runs the production enrichment prompt on 10 homepages (latency and SiteGuess schema validity) and the full `extract_invoice` pipeline with the repair pass on the 6 labelled sample invoices in `evals/data/invoices` (exact-match against labels, status against expected status, latency, tokens). It accepts a comma-separated `--models` list, `--skip-enrich`, `--skip-extract` and `--page-chars`. It does not test tool calling, so that needs a small addition. Recommended steps:

1. Pull `qwen3.5:4b`, `qwen3:4b-instruct-2507`, `granite4:3b` (plus a 4.1 tag if one exists), and two challengers, `phi4-mini` and `ministral-3:3b`. Keep your recorded `granite4.2:3b` runs as the baseline.
2. Set `OLLAMA_FLASH_ATTENTION=1` and `OLLAMA_KV_CACHE_TYPE=q8_0`. Earlier results were recorded against Ollama at `http://ollama:11434/v1` (a container); Docker/WSL2 overhead on this card is unquantified, so re-run natively if GPU passthrough is a variable.
3. Run `python -m evals.bench_llm --models qwen3.5:4b,qwen3:4b-instruct-2507,granite4:3b,phi4-mini,ministral-3:3b` and compare field exact-match, status agreement, schema-valid count, and p50/max latency. For Qwen3.5, test with thinking off and confirm no thinking tokens leak and the schema is enforced (the ollama#15260 bug class).
4. Grow the labelled set before trusting differences. Six invoices cannot separate models within a few points; add 30-50 noisy and multi-line invoices, plus a few tool-call prompts with several candidate tools and a multi-turn case, since multi-turn is where small models fail.
5. Record `ollama ps` (GPU vs CPU split) and VRAM during each run, since a silent spill explains conflicting speed numbers like 16.7 vs 9.6 t/s. Add a Q5 or Q6 build of the winner if a tag exists.
6. Only afterward test one stretch candidate, Qwen3-8B Q3_K_M (4.12 GB) or a 3-bit Granite 4.1 8B at 4k context, with a `llama-bench` `-ngl` sweep for speed.

## Conclusion

The question shifts from "which model is smartest" to "which small model stays correct at the tail." On this card the 3-4B class at Q4 or better is the sweet spot: the vendor-reported gap to an 8B (Granite 4.1 3B vs 8B: BFCL v3 60.80 vs 68.27) is plausibly smaller than the penalty from 3-bit quantization or CPU spill on a model that must emit exact JSON, though no head-to-head test of that trade-off exists. Constrained decoding converts the structured-output problem into a field-accuracy problem that no public benchmark answers for invoices.

Treat Qwen3.5-4B as the ceiling candidate, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 as the default to beat because it holds the only independent structured-output evidence, and Granite 4.1 3B as the tool-calling and repo-continuity hedge. If your labelled invoices show all three within noise, pick the one with the lowest p95 latency and fewest repair-pass invocations, since throughput on a 1660 is the resource you cannot buy back.
